# Decision audit

Classify the unknowns as verifiable, preference, or settled by explicit scope. Probe verifiable facts that could change the choice. Send consequential user preferences through ddh-direct intake before choosing or acting. Separate observations, inferences and unknowns. A build or file edit may confirm an intermediate step. Verify the effect at its real entry point when possible.

Compare two to four candidates, including the strongest alternative. Score fit and evidence depth separately on a 0–10 scale. A candidate can fit well while its assumptions remain weak. Require depth of at least 9 before a consequential automatic choice. Record which fact supports or rules out each candidate.

For a reversible local choice with a known target, saved prior state, a one-step rollback and an observable result, read [the review procedure](references/review.md) and use its self-review path. Use a fresh-context reviewer for shared or higher-impact choices if authorized and supported. Give it facts and options, withhold your preferred result, and wait using the host's lifecycle. Report a missing reviewer and keep the decision pending when independent review is required.

Escalate when an unresolved preference decides the outcome, the evidence remains below the bar, or the reviewed rating margin is below 2. Obtain explicit authorization for a shared or hard-to-reverse action. Irreversible actions require a concrete user-authorized target and action. Host permissions still apply.

Before acting, state the target and check. Observe the outcome and record the candidate scores, material evidence, review or self-review, approval boundary and outcome where future work depends on them. Preserve rejected approaches that would otherwise be retried.

This review challenges the choice before action. Verification after execution checks the resulting artifact. Reuse sufficient prior evidence and request another independent review only when it resolves a remaining question.
